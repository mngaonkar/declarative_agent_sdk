The Adventure of the Speckled Band — Summary

Helen Stoner visits Sherlock Holmes because she fears for her life. Her sister Julia died two years earlier under mysterious circumstances just before her wedding. Julia’s last words referred to a “speckled band,” but no one understood what she meant.

Helen explains that she and Julia lived with their violent stepfather, Dr. Grimesby Roylott, at Stoke Moran. Roylott is a frightening man with a terrible temper, and he keeps unusual animals on the property, including a cheetah and a baboon. Now that repairs are being done to the house, Helen has been moved into Julia’s old bedroom. She has begun hearing the same strange whistle in the night that Julia heard before she died.

Holmes and Watson travel to Stoke Moran to inspect the rooms. Holmes notices several suspicious details: the ventilator in Julia’s room opens into Roylott’s room instead of outside, the bell-rope is fake and does not ring, and the bed is clamped to the floor so it cannot be moved. In Roylott’s room Holmes finds a saucer of milk, a dog lash tied into a loop, and a safe.

That night Holmes and Watson wait in the dark inside Helen’s room. After hours of silence, Holmes hears a soft sound and sees a snake coming through the ventilator and down the bell-rope. Holmes strikes at it with his cane, forcing it back into Roylott’s room. The snake then bites Roylott, killing him.

Holmes explains that Roylott trained the snake to crawl through the ventilator and down the rope, using the whistle to call it back and the milk as part of the training. Roylott’s motive was money: if Helen or Julia married, he would lose access to income connected to their inheritance. Julia had been murdered this way, and Helen was about to suffer the same fate.

The story is one of Holmes’s most famous cases because it turns ordinary household details into clues for a clever and horrifying murder method.
